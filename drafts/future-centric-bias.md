### Overview
Rationalist and LessWrong communities champion rigorous reasoning, but a tiny logical slip—over‑weighting an extremely speculative future—can cascade into catastrophic policies. When a small fallacy enters an otherwise sensible utilitarian chain, it justifies any means to achieve a post‑scarcity utopia, treating present‑day suffering as expendable.

### How a Small Fallacy Escalates
1. **Assume a non‑zero chance of a trillion‑human future** → treat expected utility as `p × 10^12` lives. Even with `p = 0.0001` the product dwarfs any current welfare concern.
2. **Linear aggregation of utility** → add that massive expected value to today’s actions.
3. **Ignore epistemic uncertainty** → treat the probability as a hard fact, effectively discounting present suffering to zero.
4. **Derive policy** → any intervention that marginally raises `p` (e.g., extreme AI‑alignment work, radical bio‑enhancement) is deemed morally obligatory, regardless of immediate costs.

### Real‑World Examples of Disastrous Outcomes
- **AI‑Risk Prioritization** – Billions flow to speculative AI‑safety labs while proven global‑health interventions (malaria nets, clean water) receive a fraction of the funding, prolonging millions of preventable deaths.
- **Cryonics & Longevity Funding** – Charitable donations are diverted from poverty alleviation to preserve a handful of wealthy individuals for a hoped‑for future, amplifying inequality today.
- **Existential‑Risk Laws** – Governments consider draconian surveillance or bans on research on the premise that any chance of preventing a future catastrophe outweighs current civil‑rights harms.
- **LLM‑Hype Magical Thinking** – Tech firms promise that ever‑scaling language models will “solve everything,” justifying massive compute farms while ignoring physical limits; policies built on this assumption misallocate resources.
- **Population‑Control Policies** – Some futurist proposals endorse severe reproductive restrictions today, betting on a future where post‑scarcity makes human labor obsolete.
- **Resource‑Intensive AI Compute** – Investment in giant GPU farms consumes rare minerals and energy, exacerbating climate change while promising marginal future AI benefits.
- **Moral Outrage Campaigns** – Online movements rally against present‑day injustices by framing them as obstacles to a utopian future, diverting attention from concrete remedies.

### Why the Fallacy Persists
- **Pascalian Expected Value** – Treating a minuscule probability as if it were certain because the payoff is astronomically large.
- **Conjunction Fallacy of “Trillion‑Human” Scenarios** – Assuming that if both a transformative technology and a massive future exist, they must dominate moral calculus.
- **Magical Scaling Assumptions** – Believing performance will continue to improve logarithmically forever, ignoring hardware and thermodynamic constraints.

### Toward a Balanced Approach
- **Dual‑Horizon Allocation** – Split resources between high‑uncertainty, high‑impact future work *and* low‑uncertainty, high‑impact present interventions.
- **Calibrated Discounting** – Weight expected utility by epistemic confidence; a 0.001 % chance should be heavily discounted compared to a 99 % chance of current suffering.
- **Humility in Forecasting** – Recognize the limits of scaling laws for LLMs and the speculative nature of post‑scarcity scenarios.
